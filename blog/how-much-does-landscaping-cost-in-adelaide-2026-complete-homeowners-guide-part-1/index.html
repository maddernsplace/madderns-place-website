How Much Does Landscaping Cost in Adelaide? (2026 Complete Homeowner's Guide) Part 1

Blog Post
📅 25 June 2026

How Much Does Landscaping Cost in Adelaide?

One of the first questions we hear from homeowners is:

"How much is landscaping going to cost me?"

It's a great question, but it's one that's almost impossible to answer without seeing the property first.

Over the years, we've completed landscaping projects all across Southern Adelaide—from smaller lawn renovations in Reynella and Morphett Vale to complete front and rear yard transformations in Seaford, Moana, Aldinga Beach, Port Noarlunga, Happy Valley, Woodcroft, Hallett Cove, McLaren Vale and many other suburbs.

Some jobs have taken a single day and cost only a few thousand dollars.

Others have involved weeks of excavation, retaining walls, drainage, irrigation, paving and premium landscaping finishes.

The final cost always comes down to one thing:

Every property is different.

A flat block with good soil and easy machine access will almost always cost less than a steep property with poor drainage, difficult access and clay soil.

Unfortunately, many websites give landscaping prices that are far too simplistic. They'll often say something like, "Landscaping costs between $50 and $150 per square metre."

While that's technically true in some situations, it doesn't tell the whole story.

Landscaping isn't like buying carpet or paint where you simply multiply the area by a price.

Every project has its own challenges, and much of the work happens before you even see the finished result.

This guide is designed to give Adelaide homeowners a realistic understanding of where their money goes, what affects pricing, and how to avoid expensive mistakes.

Average Landscaping Costs in Adelaide

Although every project is different, the following price ranges provide a realistic guide for residential landscaping across Adelaide.

Landscaping ProjectTypical Price Range
Lawn installation$3,000 – $8,000
Irrigation system$1,500 – $6,000
Garden makeover$2,000 – $10,000
Retaining wall$3,000 – $20,000+
Paving$3,000 – $15,000+
Front yard makeover$5,000 – $20,000
Complete backyard transformation$8,000 – $40,000+
Full front & rear landscaping$15,000 – $60,000+

These figures are intended as a guide only.

For example, we've completed front lawn renovations in Moana for well under $5,000, while larger sloping properties in Hallett Cove requiring retaining walls, drainage and multiple levels have been significantly more.

That's why every professional landscaper should inspect the property before providing an accurate quote.

Why Landscaping Prices Can Vary So Much

One question we often get asked is:

"My neighbour paid $6,000. Why is my quote different?"

The answer usually comes down to what's happening below the surface.

Two properties may look almost identical from the street, but once excavation begins they can become completely different projects.

For example, we've worked on properties throughout Seaford Rise, Aldinga Beach and Port Noarlunga South where we discovered:

  • Broken concrete buried beneath the lawn
  • Large rocks hidden under the soil
  • Builder's rubble
  • Old irrigation pipes
  • Clay so hard machinery struggled to dig through
  • Poor drainage causing constant waterlogging

None of these problems are obvious until work begins.

This is one reason why experienced landscapers spend time inspecting your property before providing a quote.

It's also why extremely cheap quotes can sometimes leave important work out altogether.

Site Preparation – The Foundation of Every Great Landscape

If there's one stage that determines whether a landscaping project succeeds or fails, it's site preparation.

Unfortunately, it's also the part homeowners rarely see once the job is finished.

When someone looks at a beautiful new lawn, they see lush green grass.

What they don't see is everything underneath it.

Proper preparation may include:

  • Removing existing lawn
  • Spraying and treating weeds
  • Excavating old soil
  • Removing rocks and rubble
  • Levelling uneven areas
  • Importing quality lawn soil
  • Installing drainage
  • Compacting foundations
  • Establishing correct falls away from buildings

Skipping these steps may reduce the quote today, but it often leads to expensive problems later.

Think of landscaping like building a house.

You wouldn't build on poor foundations.

The same principle applies outdoors.

The Hidden Cost of Builder's Fill

One thing we've noticed working throughout Southern Adelaide is how common builder's fill has become on newer housing developments.

Suburbs such as:

  • Seaford Meadows
  • Aldinga Beach
  • Seaford Rise
  • Andrews Farm
  • Virginia
  • Angle Vale

often contain compacted clay, rocks and construction waste beneath the top layer of soil.

It isn't unusual to uncover:

  • Bricks
  • Concrete
  • Plastic
  • Roofing materials
  • Large rocks
  • Excess clay

Many homeowners assume their lawn failed because they chose the wrong fertiliser.

In reality, the problem started years earlier when the lawn was installed over poor-quality soil.

Replacing unsuitable material with premium lawn underlay can make an enormous difference to drainage, root development and long-term lawn health.

Although this preparation adds to the initial cost, it usually saves money over the life of the lawn.

Excavation and Earthworks

Excavation is another area where landscaping costs can vary considerably.

Some projects require only minor levelling.

Others involve removing 20 tonnes or more of soil before landscaping can even begin.

Excavation may be required to:

  • Correct drainage problems
  • Remove unsuitable soil
  • Lower finished levels
  • Prepare retaining walls
  • Install irrigation
  • Create level lawn areas
  • Prepare paving foundations

The amount of soil removed directly affects labour, machinery hire and disposal costs.

One of the biggest misconceptions is that soil can simply be "spread somewhere else."

Unfortunately, many suburban properties don't have suitable locations to relocate excavated material, meaning it must be loaded into trucks and disposed of at licensed facilities.

That disposal cost is often overlooked when homeowners compare landscaping quotes.

Soil Quality Matters More Than Most People Realise

Healthy landscaping starts with healthy soil.

It's something many people don't think about until their lawn begins to struggle.

Across suburbs like Happy Valley, Woodcroft, Flagstaff Hill and Reynella, we regularly encounter heavy clay soils.

Clay isn't necessarily bad.

In fact, it contains valuable nutrients.

The problem is that compacted clay drains poorly.

Water struggles to penetrate, roots can't spread easily and lawns often become waterlogged during winter before drying rock-hard during summer.

Depending on the property, improving soil may involve:

  • Premium lawn underlay
  • Organic compost
  • Gypsum applications
  • Sand blending
  • Soil conditioners
  • Rotary hoeing existing soil

Choosing the right soil preparation method depends on the site's existing conditions.

There is no single solution that works for every property.

New Lawn Installation Costs

Installing new turf remains one of the most popular landscaping projects throughout Adelaide.

A fresh lawn immediately transforms the appearance of almost any property.

It creates usable outdoor space, improves street appeal and gives children and pets somewhere to play.

Most professionally installed lawns fall somewhere between $3,000 and $8,000, although larger properties or premium turf selections may exceed this range.

Several factors influence the final cost.

Lawn Size

The obvious factor is the size of the area.

Larger lawns require:

  • More soil
  • More turf
  • More labour
  • More irrigation
  • Longer installation time

However, size isn't everything.

A small backyard with difficult access may actually cost more per square metre than a large open front lawn where machinery can work efficiently.

Choosing the Right Turf

One of the questions we get asked almost every week is:

"What's the best grass for Adelaide?"

The honest answer is that it depends on how you use your yard.

Kikuyu

Kikuyu is one of the most popular choices across Southern Adelaide.

It's fast growing, durable and recovers quickly from wear.

For families with children, pets or busy backyards, it's often an excellent option.

Homeowners in Seaford, Moana, Morphett Vale and Port Noarlunga regularly choose Kikuyu because it provides outstanding value while handling heavy use extremely well.

Buffalo

Buffalo varieties are popular for homeowners wanting a softer lawn underfoot.

They're generally slower growing than Kikuyu, require less frequent mowing and perform well in areas receiving partial shade.

This makes them a popular choice for established gardens in suburbs such as Happy Valley, Woodcroft and Hallett Cove, where mature trees are common.

Couch

Couch grass is often chosen for its fine leaf and manicured appearance.

When maintained properly, it produces an exceptional finish.

However, it generally requires more frequent mowing and ongoing maintenance than Kikuyu or Buffalo.

For homeowners seeking a golf-course style lawn and who enjoy regular lawn care, Couch can be an excellent choice.

Does Irrigation Increase Landscaping Costs?

Yes—but in most cases it's money well spent.

Installing premium turf without a properly designed irrigation system is a little like buying a new car and never servicing it.

A professionally designed irrigation system helps protect your investment from day one.

Throughout Adelaide's hot, dry summers, consistent watering is essential for establishing healthy lawns and gardens.

The cost of irrigation depends on factors such as:

  • Number of watering zones
  • Property size
  • Water pressure
  • Garden bed requirements
  • Controller type
  • Pipework
  • Sprinkler selection

For most residential homes, irrigation systems generally range from $1,500 to $6,000, with larger or more complex properties exceeding this range.
