Landscaping That Works in Hallett Cove

Hallett Cove is one of Adelaide's most visually striking suburbs — elevated streets with sea views, the conservation park on the doorstep, and a coastal character that sets it apart from most of Adelaide's south. Landscaping here needs to account for the sea breeze exposure, higher summer evaporation rates, and the range of soil types that exist across the suburb from the beachfront to the elevated ridge.

We've landscaped properties right across Hallett Cove — from new builds in the streets near the conservation park through to garden renovations on established properties closer to the coast. Every project is designed with the local conditions in mind: plant selection that handles salt-laden winds, irrigation calibrated for Hallett Cove's faster soil drying, and retaining walls engineered for the sloped terrain that characterises many streets in the upper part of the suburb.

From the first site visit through to final cleanup, we handle everything in-house. Turf, irrigation, retaining walls, drainage, planting, and gutter cleaning — one team, one point of contact, and a finished result that holds up in Hallett Cove's conditions year after year.

Local Knowledge

Why Hallett Cove Landscaping Demands Coastal Know-How

Hallett Cove's coastal location creates a distinct set of challenges that make good landscaping design genuinely different here compared to inland suburbs. The consistent sea breeze — pleasant for residents, tough on gardens — carries salt that accumulates on leaf surfaces and in soil, stressing plants that aren't selected with this in mind. We choose species known to handle coastal exposure, and position plantings to provide windbreak shelter for more sensitive plants within the garden.

Summer evaporation in Hallett Cove is noticeably higher than in sheltered suburban areas further inland. Lawns and garden beds dry out faster, which means irrigation systems need to be both properly sized and correctly programmed. An irrigation system designed for a Morphett Vale property will underperform in Hallett Cove's more exposed conditions — which is why we assess each site's specific microclimate before designing a system, not after.

The terrain across Hallett Cove varies considerably. Streets near the beachfront are relatively flat with sandy soils, while the elevated sections of the suburb overlooking the conservation park have heavier soils and more pronounced slopes. Retaining walls are common on these upper streets, and they need to be built with footings and drainage suited to the specific ground conditions on that part of the hill — not a generic approach.

Newer homes in Hallett Cove, particularly in the streets closer to the conservation park, often come with a blank block and an opportunity to do it right from the start. We work with these new homeowners to build complete outdoor spaces — turf, irrigation, garden beds, planting, and mulching — in a way that's designed to establish quickly and thrive in the local conditions without ongoing problems in the first few summers.
